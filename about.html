The Problem

Built because nothing else was good enough.

It started with a simple, embarrassing problem: buying a record at a shop, getting home, and realizing you already own it. Again.

Discogs is the gold standard for serious collectors — but it's exhaustively specific about release dates and pressings, built for trading and cataloguing rather than day-to-day collecting. The alternative was a spreadsheet, which meant squinting at a phone in a dusty record shop trying to search an Excel file. Manual, tedious, and prone to exactly the kind of error that costs you money.

Spin Vinyl was built to solve that. A barcode scan in the aisle tells you instantly whether the record in your hand is already on your shelf — and if it isn't, it shows you the label, year, and what it's selling for on Discogs right now.

The Vision

Not a database. A living room.

"Discogs is the spreadsheet. Spin Vinyl is the living room."

A vinyl collection is never just a list of records. It's a self-portrait — shaped by obsessions, chance discoveries, emotional moments, and decades of taste. Spin Vinyl is built to reflect that.

Your Music DNA shows you what your shelves say about you. Your Listening Room puts your favourite covers on the wall. Your Trophies mark the finds worth bragging about. And your profile is something you'd actually want to share with another collector.

The bigger dream is a community — passionate listeners and collectors sharing their love of music with each other. Not for money, not for fame. Just for the love of it.

The Creator

Hi, I'm Trey.

I've been a lover of music for decades, but a vinyl collector for only about six months. It turns out those six months were enough to build a 600-record collection and a piece of software to go with it.

By day I work in strategy and operations consulting. By nature I'm an amateur musician — a student of guitar, piano, and harmonica — with the specific and slightly unrealistic dream of becoming a showstopper Delta Blues guitarist. The records on my shelves are the education I never got in a classroom.

For me, a vinyl record is the ultimate intersection of history, music, art, technology, economics, and culture — pressed into twelve inches of black wax. Collecting them is a way of paying attention to all of it at once.
